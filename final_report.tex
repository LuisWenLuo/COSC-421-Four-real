\begin{abstract}
This report synthesizes four analyses of the Canadian air transportation network, integrating structural connectivity, centrality metrics, delay propagation, weighted and unweighted network modeling, and robustness simulations. Toronto (YYZ), Calgary (YYC), and Vancouver (YVR) emerge as dominant hubs, with Montreal (YUL) and Edmonton (YEG) forming secondary tiers. Findings highlight: hub-and-spoke structure, correlation between centrality and traffic volume, nuanced relationships with delay propagation, and high vulnerability to targeted removal of major hubs. All code and datasets are available at \url{https://github.com/LuisWenLuo/COSC-421-Four-real}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Canada's vast geography and uneven population distribution make air transportation essential for national connectivity. This report integrates four complementary analyses:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
    \item Structural centrality and traffic volume.
    \item Structural centrality and delay propagation.
    \item Weighted directed network construction and robustness simulations.
    \item Undirected weighted analysis of connectivity and hub structure.
\end{enumerate}

Collectively, these analyses provide a detailed network-science perspective on the functioning, vulnerability, and operational patterns of Canada’s major airports.

\section{Robustness Simulation}

Results indicate:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Removing YYZ produces immediate increases in average path length.
    \item Removing YYC and YVR further fragments the network.
    \item The network is robust to random failures but vulnerable to targeted disruption.
\end{itemize}

\section{Undirected Connectivity Structure}
The undirected weighted network shows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Low edge density and multiple disconnected components.
    \item Degree homogeneity but strong weighted heterogeneity.
    \item Clear hub-and-spoke architecture driven by traffic volume.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item YYZ, YYC, and YVR form the structural core of Canada’s air network.
    \item Centrality strongly correlates with flight volume and delay volume.
    \item Delay propagation rates do not strongly correlate with centrality.
    \item Major hubs are critical points of failure in robustness simulations.
    \item Peripheral airports dampen delays more reliably than central hubs.
    \item Weighted analysis reveals hub dominance overlooked in unweighted topology.
\end{enumerate}
